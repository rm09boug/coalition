*The Cradle of Culture*

Verdant valleys and misty woodlands known for poets, artisans, and the oldest noble bloodlines.  [[03. LOCATIONS/The Vale of Rhaenne]] houses Aurenne’s academies and conservatories, preserving art and etiquette from the old monarchy. Its countess safeguards tradition even as the world around her shifts toward reform.
### Cities

#### Eredhal
_The Swan City_  
A lakeside jewel of marble colonnades and mirrored waters, Eredhal is famed for its poetry salons and moonlit regattas. Here, nobles commission operas and duels of wit with equal passion. Its harbor, lined with silver-limbed willows, reflects the moon like glass.
#### Marivelle
_The Painter’s Haven_  
Built where the vale’s streams spill into the lake, Marivelle is a labyrinth of bridges, gardens, and terraced studios. Painters here grind pigments from the valley’s flowers and sell them across Aurenne. The air smells faintly of oil and lilac, and no two shutters are the same color.
#### Tarethien
_The Scholar’s Hill_  
Rising on the northern rim of the vale, Tarethien hosts the Conservatory of Saint Auren—a bastion of learning and music older than the crown itself. Bells toll from its ivory spires at dawn and dusk, marking the rhythm of scholarly life below.
#### Caer Auren
_The Watch Upon the Water_  
Northwest of the capital, Caer Auren rises on a rocky bluff overlooking the Argent Bay. Once a border fortress that guarded the northern approaches to Aurenne City, it has since grown into a thriving harbor for naval engineers and shipwrights. Drydocks line the sheltered inlet below, where sleek galleons and patrol frigates take shape beneath banners of the crown. The city’s citadel still keeps vigil over the bay, its beacon tower a silent reminder that the peace of Aurenne was earned through centuries of vigilance.
#### Verrithen
_The City of Rivers_  
Set amid green floodplains north of Argent Bay, Verrithen sprawls along the meeting of three rivers that twist together like silver threads. Once a fishing town, it flourished when the royal canals linked it to the capital, transforming it into a vital artery of inland trade. Great waterwheels churn beside warehouses and bridges draped in ivy, while its markets hum with merchants from every province. Verrithen’s people are practical and sharp-tongued, proud of their wealth and wary of the court’s soft hands, they say “the rivers pays no mind to crowns.”
#### Selvene
_The Veiled Peninsula_  
Where the Vale of Rhaenne’s western hills lean into the sea, Selvene perches on a slender peninsula wrapped in silver fog and salt wind. Once a retreat for poets and scholars seeking solitude, it has grown into a haven of quiet wealth, its alabaster villas and weathered spires rising between gardens of myrtle and stone-pine. The sea mists that roll in each morning lend the city its name and its mystery; from afar, Selvene appears to drift above the waves like a dream half-remembered.
#### Varosk
_The Stormbound Gate_  
Where the northern sea hammers against basalt cliffs, Varosk endures. The city began as a chain of signal towers guarding the ice routes, now knit together by walls of dark stone and ironwood. Its harbors are narrow and deep, cut straight into the rock to shield ships from the gales that scour the coast. Smoke from the forges and salt from the waves mingle in the air, giving the city its sharp, metallic scent.

Varosk’s people are mariners and iron-workers both—tough, close-lipped, and superstitious of the sea’s northern moods. They build ships with double hulls to brave the frigid current and still sing odes to Kaerath in the long nights, believing the Duskwarden watches over their voyages. To the rest of Aurenne, Varosk is a frontier of endurance—the first to meet the storm and the last to yield to it.
#### Myr Dhalas
_The Frozen Strand_  
East along the northern unforgiving coast lies Myr Dhalas, a low-stone port clinging to a wind-carved fjord. Once an outpost for trappers and whale-hunters, it has become a vital supply harbor for northern expeditions. Its houses crouch close together around steaming bathhouses fed by hidden springs, and its streets glow with the light of oil-lamps made from the very whales it once hunted.

The citizens of Myr Dhalas speak in low, melodic cadences, their accent softened by the hush of snow and surf. They trade in whale-bone, seal pelts, and a rare frostglass, crystals gathered from caves beneath the frozen cliffs. In the brief summer, the sea blooms with color and song; in winter, the city seems carved from the very ice that surrounds it, an ember of life in a world half-asleep.
