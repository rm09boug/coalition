Hailing from the City of Ramona within the [[Ashonti Desert]], the Darakan clan was primarily known for the esteemed military leadership of [[General Y’uin Darakan]], the head of the clan at the time. The clan’s early family history paints a picture of battlefield prodigies and broad rumors of ancestors celebrated for heroisms. It was not until several generations later that trade established itself in the family, perhaps as tides of strife settled and peace opened doors for a new prospect. Darakan nobility rose to a respected popularity, benefiting from their clan’s long-standing reputation and the quality of their merchandise. The house evolved as the times did, collecting literature, art, material techniques, languages, and cultural prowess to invest in future generations of astute scholars and warriors. 

To move from the city of Ramona to [[03. LOCATIONS/Aurenne]] was an act some deemed foolish and others believed was ingenious. While the real reason behind the decision is left to speculation, it is at least apparent that the clan had a fair standing with the royal kingdom of Aurenne, granting them land between [[03. LOCATIONS/The Silver Vale]] and [[03. LOCATIONS/The Deyrholm Frontier]], to be what is now named Ramona’s Mirror, the dukedom of House Darakan. They would also officially brand their spices, fragrances, candles, and soaps as goods of the [[Maharasa Syndicate]].

Nasrin, born of the first generation to have settled in Aurenne, feels great pride in his family’s history and teachings. He holds the position of Administrator of the Maharasa Syndicate, tasked not with closing agreements over a table, but with processing the lengthy and dull paperwork that comes after.

Nasrin obeys without hesitation, doing what he is told, when he is told. The most loyal of the Darakan sons, he is also the most overlooked. His efforts to distinguish himself in his father’s eyes often feel futile, as though some unspoken flaw renders him untrusted or unwanted. Still, he persists.

Aspiring to become a martial warrior like his grandfather, his former mentor, Nasrin doubled every lesson, mastering twice what his elder brother was taught in hopes of earning greater responsibility and, perhaps, a measure of respect. The recognition he sought eventually came, though not in the form he imagined: an arranged marriage, a role he accepted without hesitation. Yet a quiet sting remains. A reminder that, no matter how hard he strives, his station will always fall beneath his brother’s.
