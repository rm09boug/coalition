His clothing is fine but never ostentatious: polished boots, pressed shirts and a deep sapphire coat that hints at the family fleet without flaunting it.
**Disposition:** Ventus’s demeanor is disciplined, practical, and composed, the sort of man who measures twice and cuts once. He carries himself with the authority of a noble who knows his roots, firmly rejecting the arrogance so many of his peers display. He speaks directly, treats people with a cool fairness, and expects his orders to be followed the first time, not because he demands obedience but because he plans carefully. Though he never took to the sailor’s life – finding no love for the rolling decks or the unpredictability of the tide – he proved indispensable on land.

### About
Where his father [[04. WORLD/House Lyell\|Vander Lyell]] built the family’s rise with daring raids and wild instinct, and where his son Vesper courts adventure with a grin and a blade, Ventus is the quiet spine of the Lyell name: the man who keeps the house standing when the tides turn rough.

He became infatuated with Aurora Blackwood, the middle daughter of a somewhat influential winemaking family. Their joining was not one of obligation, though it did have its benefits for both families, concerning the export of Blackwood wines. The pair would have four children together and maintain an affectionate relationship for the entirety of their days.

Ventus became the architect of the Lyell fleet’s legitimacy, tirelessly drafting charters, building alliances, forging contracts, and smoothing the rough image that corsair bloodshed had etched into the family legacy. He is the one who deals with envoys, merchants, nobles, and naval officials–a role he handles with razor-sharp tact and unshakeable composure. 
His work ethic is relentless. His standards high. He expects excellence from himself and from every Lyell under his roof, his eldest son most of all. Ventus often finds himself exasperated with Vesper’s antics, chastising him for his conduct in public.

Yet beneath the disciplined exterior lies a deep loyalty and a quiet protectiveness. He is not cold; simply reserved. A man who shows affection through provision, not poetry. He does not brag about his family, but his eyes soften whenever their achievements are spoken of.
